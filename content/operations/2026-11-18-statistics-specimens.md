## MG-261118-S01 — GCSE Higher / Grove Weekly (4 marks)
A survey records the number of repairs required by 10 bicycles. Two require 0 repairs, three require 1 and five require 2. Calculate the mean and the percentage requiring at least one repair.
Solution: total repairs = 2×0+3×1+5×2=13. Mean=13/10=1.3. Eight of ten bicycles require at least one repair; 80%.
Proposed scheme: M1 weighted total; A1 1.3; M1 8/10; A1 80%. Accept repeated addition. Check: expanded list 0,0,1,1,1,2,2,2,2,2 sums to 13.

## MG-261118-S02 — Cambridge International AS Mathematics 9709, probability and statistics / Grove Daily (5 marks)
A component independently fails a test with probability 0.25. Four components are tested. Find (a) the probability exactly one fails (b) the probability at least one fails.
Solution: X~B(4,0.25). (a) 4×0.25×0.75³=0.421875. (b) 1−0.75⁴=0.68359375.
Proposed scheme: (a) M1 product for a specified sequence; M1 four possible positions; A1 0.421875; (b) M1 complement of zero failures; A1 0.68359375. Accept equivalent exact fractions or 3 s.f. (0.422, 0.684). Alternative check: enumerate 16 outcomes; the four one-failure outcomes are equiprobable.

## MG-261118-S03 — Pearson Edexcel International A Level Mathematics, statistics candidate / Grove Exam open preparation (5 marks)
A fictional factory uses two machines. Machine A makes 30% of its products and machine B makes the rest. A product from A passes an inspection with probability 0.8 and one from B passes with probability 0.1. A randomly selected product passes. Find the probability that A made it.
Solution: P(A∩pass)=0.3×0.8=0.24; P(B∩pass)=0.7×0.1=0.07; P(pass)=0.31; P(A|pass)=0.24/0.31=24/31.
Proposed scheme: B1 P(B)=0.7; M1 branch products; A1 total 0.31; M1 divide joint probability by total; A1 24/31 (0.774 to 3 s.f.). Alternative check: out of a hypothetical 1000 products, expect 240 A passes and 70 B passes.
Text tree: A (0.30) → pass (0.80), not pass (0.20); B (0.70) → pass (0.10), not pass (0.90). Branches sum to one. No image necessary.
